\begin{abstract}
Products of even and odd functions, and their integrals over an interval symmetric about $0$. Textbook §5.5.
\end{abstract}
\maketitle

Recall that a function is even if $f(-x)=f(x)$ and odd if $f(-x)=-f(x)$. Some examples:

\begin{theorem}[Properties of even and odd functions]\label{theorem:13-12}
If $f_{1}(x)$ and $f_{2}(x)$ are even functions and if $g_{1}(x)$ and $g_{2}(x)$ are odd functinos, then, where the following operations are defined, we have:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item $f_{1}(x) f_{2}(x)$ and $g_{1}(x) g_{2}(x)$ are \wordChoice{\choice[correct]{even}\choice{odd}\choice{neither even nor odd}} functions.
  \item $f_{1}(x) g_{1}(x)$ is an \wordChoice{\choice[correct]{odd}\choice{even}\choice{neither even nor odd}} function.
  \item $\int_{-a}^{a} f_{1}(x) d x=\answer{2 \int_{0}^{a} f_{1}(x) d x}$
  \item $\int_{-a}^{a} g_{1}(x) d x=\answer{0}$
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\begin{example}\label{example:13-13}
For example:
% work: x^2 sin(x) is even*odd = odd, x^7 is odd, x cos(x) is odd*even = odd; the integrand is odd so the integral over [-a,a] is 0
\[
\int_{-a}^{a} x^{2} \sin (x)-x^{7}+x \cos (x) d x=\answer{0}
\]
\end{example}
